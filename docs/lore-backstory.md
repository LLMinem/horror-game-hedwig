# Lore and backstory: Uncle and Hedwig

**Provisional creative draft.** Reframed on 2026-10-22 from the original story document dated 2025-09-03. The [glossary](../GLOSSARY.md) owns the accepted character identities. Everything beyond those identities below remains a creative possibility, not confirmed canon, implemented behavior or a milestone requirement.

## Family and tragic relationship

The player could be the Uncle's nephew or niece. That family tie would make avoiding him emotionally complicated: the danger comes from someone familiar, not an anonymous pursuer.

In this draft, Hedwig was the only love of the Uncle's life. Their marriage was loving but toxic: she was controlling, and he depended completely on her for stability and direction. He feared her but could not leave. The relationship offered security at the cost of his autonomy; he could not imagine functioning without her.

The draft places his severe decline after her death. He visits her grave repeatedly but cannot remember where it is, wandering between graves in a desperate search. His mumbled words sound distantly familiar to the player without becoming clear. Whether he recognizes the player as family remains unresolved. These are fictional narrative proposals, not medical claims about dementia or grief.

Hedwig's presence could continue that controlling relationship after death. His danger would arise from love and desperation rather than an intention to attack the player. The emotional aim is empathy without removing tension: grief, dependency, loss of autonomy and family connection make survival personally difficult.

## Original gameplay ideas, still provisional

The earlier draft imagined watering eight flower-covered graves while avoiding the Uncle. Completing the watering would end the task; getting too close to him would cause a loss. He would pause at graves to search, then move on, with audible mumbling helping the player track him. This preserves a creative premise, not a claim that this loop exists or that eight graves is an approved requirement.

A second idea contrasts two kinds of behavior:

- **Confused searching:** repetitive wandering between graves, unclear speech and comparatively predictable movement.
- **Seeking Hedwig:** her appearance briefly gives him a focused destination. He moves directly toward her, navigating around buildings rather than continuing his usual route. The motive is longing, not aggression toward the player.

The draft imagines Hedwig appearing or teleporting unpredictably across the cemetery. As the player waters more graves, she could appear more often or become more dangerous. Seeing her would redirect the Uncle, potentially trapping the player between them. The player would need to remember his whereabouts and anticipate how her appearances change the danger.

The contrast between habitual routes and seeking Hedwig is a narrative behavior idea, not an invisible path-corridor restriction on player movement. No AI state machine, pathfinding method or trigger implementation is prescribed here.

## Tone and unresolved creative questions

Repetitive searching, uncertainty about family recognition and brief moments of focus could carry the psychological horror. The marriage could be portrayed as both nurturing and controlling; even after death, its power imbalance shapes the Uncle's actions. His inability to process the loss is the tragic core of this particular draft.

The desired tension comes from unpredictability and divided attention rather than only direct pursuit. The Uncle should remain threatening without becoming frustrating; Hedwig's appearances should feel unpredictable but fair. Cornering should be avoidable through awareness and skill. The family relationship should add emotional weight without dissolving the horror.

These aims and depictions remain open to refinement, especially how to portray dementia, dependency and grief. They do not authorize character production or gameplay work.
